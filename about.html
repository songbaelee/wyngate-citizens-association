About the Wyngate Citizens Association

The Wyngate Citizens Association (WCA) is a nonprofit group representing the approximately 1,600 households that live between Fernwood Road, Greentree Road, the Capital Beltway, and Old Georgetown Road in Bethesda, Maryland.

The WCA works to connect neighbors, support community projects, and give residents a voice on issues affecting their quality of life. The WCA listserv — with over 1,400 members — is one of the most active neighborhood forums in the area.

Wyngate neighbors also run community groups such as Wyngate Tree Friends and Wyngate & Maplewood Neighbors Helping Neighbors. See the Community page for more.

Since 1951

WCA History

The WCA (originally called the Alta Vista Terrace – Wyngate Citizens Association) was founded in May 1951. Back then, the neighborhood was just taking shape in a very different Montgomery County. There was no Wyngate Elementary (1952), no North Bethesda Middle (1955), no Walter Johnson High (1956), no Beltway (construction began in 1957), no YMCA Bethesda (1963), and no Metro (construction began in 1969). The county had only 160,000 residents, though it had doubled in size since 1940.

Bank statements and payment receipts tell us when the WCA started, and they offer some interesting insights, even though the earliest records of communications and meeting notes date only from 1959.

  • In March 1952, the WCA took out a four-inch ad in the Bethesda-Chevy Chase Tribune for $5. The Tribune was Bethesda's first local paper and ran from 1937 to 1977.
  • The WCA rented a typewriter through most of 1951 and 1952 before buying one in October 1952 from the American Typewriter Company for $14.54. (We have no idea where that typewriter is now.)
  • In 1954, the WCA reimbursed H. Charles Ephraim for making a phone call ($1.62) and mimeographing 750 copies ($3.82).
  • In 1956, the WCA bought 173 pounds of spore powder to control a Japanese beetle infestation.
  • Every Christmas, the WCA sent donations to Wyngate, North Bethesda, and Walter Johnson.

In the 1950s, the board included some interesting positions beyond President and Vice President, such as Delegates to the North Bethesda Congress, Fire Board Representative, and Civil Defense Membership.

This is just the beginning. More of the WCA's history is coming soon.
